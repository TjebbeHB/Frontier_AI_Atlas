# What DARC/DAAP needs to become to survive the intelligence explosion — and what that costs

*Discussion brief · Shared safety project · 16 September 2026*

**Scenario assumption:** Capabilities outpace institutional understanding, competition intensifies and power concentrates. An intelligence explosion is the world being stress-tested, not a claim about today.

**The proposition.** An EU-based institution, backed by the EU, UK, Canada, Japan, South Korea and Australia, helps willing labs and US state agencies do rigorous safety work. Sponsors pay for independent audits, testing compute and agreed lab engineering time. US federal membership is not assumed; participation is hypothetical. The offer is practical help and reusable evidence, without demanding geopolitical allegiance.

## What it must become

**A standing joint investigation and response service.** Occasional reports will fall behind. The institution needs trusted access, independent judgment and the ability to help fix problems as models change.

| Requirement | What must exist before the crisis | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Trusted access** | Confidential internal-use alerts, preserved logs and secure testing agreements; lawful reporting duties remain. | Labs expose sensitive failures and commit engineers. Sponsors fund security; confidentiality limits immediate public disclosure. |
| **Ready expertise** | Paid, pre-cleared evaluators, a surge roster, reserved compute and independent replication. | Governments fund spare capacity and compete with labs for scarce talent. |
| **Adaptive repair** | Named lab decision owners; agreed containment, retesting and release gates; material model changes reopen review. | Labs accept scoped delays. Sponsors fund repeated testing and remediation support. |
| **Independent oversight** | Reviewer independence, conflict recusals, appeals, published limitations and access for smaller labs. | Sponsors and labs relinquish control over conclusions; accountability needs staff and funding. |

**The first failure is missing evidence:** an internal research agent bypasses a control, but evaluators receive only a summary—or access arrives after the model changes. Pre-agreed, funded investigation lets the lab report early, isolate the workflow and work alongside independent testers. More staff cannot fix missing access; access cannot fix missing expertise.

## Authority and limits

The partnership **requests evidence, funds investigations and administers audit agreements**. Labs authorise access and implement fixes. Compulsory disclosure and binding public-law remedies remain with competent authorities under applicable law. US state agencies participate through lawful domestic arrangements; they do not represent Washington. Cooperation grants no immunity.

If agreed gates fail, suspend the assurance claim and use applicable reporting routes. Review the pilot after **30 days**—an exercise parameter—and explicitly renew or end exceptional arrangements. Evidence-retention duties survive expiry. Nonparticipants remain outside its audit authority; passing an audit cannot guarantee safety under accelerating capabilities.

## What to budget—and what success means

**Illustrative first-year envelope: €40m, not a sourced estimate:** €15m for 60 fully loaded staff/secondment equivalents at €250,000 each; €10m for testing compute and secure infrastructure; €10m for external evaluators and lab engineering support; €5m for oversight and contingency. Validate scope, staffing and compute quotes; avoid double-counting personnel. Substantial remediation and expansion may require more.

The deeper price is sustained public subsidy, sensitive access, occasional delays and independent criticism. Judge success by **time to evidence, reproduced findings, verified repairs and coverage gaps**, rather than audit counts. Survival means staying useful and trustworthy as capabilities accelerate.

## Names to test

- **Shared AI Safety Partnership (SASP)** — preferred: collaborative, broader than audits.
- **International AI Assurance Partnership (IAAP)** — emphasises credible evidence.
- **Frontier AI Safety Service (FASS)** — foregrounds practical help.
- **Democratic AI Audit Partnership (DAAP)** — continuity, but understates the response role. **DARC**, Democratic AI Response Compact, better fits the original stress test.
